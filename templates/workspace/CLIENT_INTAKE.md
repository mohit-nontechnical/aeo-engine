## Buyer questions

List the top questions, objections, comparisons, and implementation concerns heard in sales calls. Attach call transcripts or notes where approved.

For each question, record:

- exact buyer wording when available;
- persona and buying stage;
- source call, email, search query, or internal owner;
- intended next action;
- whether an existing page already answers it.

## Defensible points of view

List three to five positions the company can defend. Name the internal owner for each.

Each point of view needs:

- one named internal owner;
- a plain statement of the position;
- the evidence or operating experience behind it;
- where it differs from market consensus, if relevant;
- public-use permission.

## Evidence room

Provide approved product documentation, policies, pricing rules, research, customer evidence, transcripts, and subject-matter experts. Do not include material that cannot be used by the service team.

For every source, provide:

- source title and stable path or URL;
- source owner;
- creation or publication date;
- date last checked;
- source type: client primary / external primary / secondary context / interview / customer evidence;
- public, internal-only, confidential, or prohibited classification;
- expiry or required recheck date when the fact can change.

Customer results, testimonials, logos, and quotations require explicit public-use permission. An account team's belief that permission exists is not enough.

## Mandatory source-sufficiency gate

A canonical brief may enter drafting only when all four are true:

1. It answers one named buyer question.
2. It contains at least one defensible client point of view with a named owner.
3. Every planned material claim has an approved claim-ledger row or named primary source.
4. There is a clear reason this client is qualified to answer the question.

If any item fails, request another interview or source, narrow the claim, label the asset as opinion where appropriate, or drop the topic. Do not fill the gap with generic web copy.

## Automatic intake blockers

- no named fact owner or final approver;
- no approved evidence for the central claim;
- requested fake testimonial, review, forum activity, customer result, or original research;
- confidential information mixed into the public source packet without permissions;
- a guarantee of ranking, citation, placement, traffic, pipeline, or revenue;
- client refuses version-specific approval while expecting publishing access;
- derivative volume is requested before the canonical source exists.

## Minimum interview output

Every recorded source interview must produce:

- transcript with speaker labels;
- three to five claim candidates;
- named opinions and their owner;
- quotations that may be used verbatim, pending approval;
- unclear or conflicting statements;
- follow-up source requests;
- proposed buyer questions and canonical formats.

The transcript is evidence input, not automatic public permission.
